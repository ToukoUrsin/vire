# Vire at the Aalto booth

Vire is a home check for people after TMS. AirPods sense head movement, nods, walking and heart rate against that person's own usual; smiles still need the camera. A short in-ear practice can answer drift, and the clinic can see who drifts and what worked. We want to test whether this simple home signal can sit beside the clinic's richer measurement.

## What we would like to check with your EEG

The founder will wear the EEG headset and AirPods Pro 3 at the same time for two minutes. Vire gives three nods on a first sync mark, then eyes open, eyes closed, one minute of slow breathing, four asked smiles, and three nods on a final sync mark. We would like to see whether the two sync marks line up cleanly, whether the eyes-closed block shows the expected alpha sanity check, and whether AirPods stillness, breathing pace and heart rate move with the EEG during slow breathing. The smiles give four simple event windows.

## What we do not claim

The AirPods do not read brain activity. Vire has no EEG in the AirPods, does not diagnose depression, and does not claim that a movement or heart-rate reading predicts a person's outcome. No patients have used this booth demonstration. It is one founder, one short run, and a sensor comparison.

## What we ask

Please give us two minutes at the booth and one export from the EEG system. The export can be the raw or minimally processed recording plus the sampling rate, channel names and marker times. Vire will provide its numbered log and a four-letter code so the files can be matched. The log contains timestamps and numbers only, with no audio or picture.
