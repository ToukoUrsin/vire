VireThe problem

A questionnaire asks how you feel. Vire measures how you react.

In lab studies, people with depression react less to good things. So once a day, Vire plays a laugh and measures the smile that answers it.

Bylsma, Morris and Rottenberg 2008: 19 laboratory studies, d = −0.53 for positive stimuli.

The big screen during a ping: the person's own face as a wireframe, and the smile line as the laughter plays. A recorded face is played into the phone's camera here.

VireThe daily ping

Your echo: four numbers against your own usual. 50 is you.

  • Tuned to your face. Five expressions in about 30 seconds train a model of your own muscle ranges, in the browser, in 44 ms.
  • Compared with you, not with everyone. Your usual is set from your own pings and stays fixed.
  • Honest about gaps. What it can't read today, it leaves out and says so.

  • The dose is chosen per person. Each one's lift is counted, measured and as you said you felt, and the next pick follows it.
  • The probe adapts too. The laugh your face answers most is used most, and another is tried now and then.
  • Small nudges, not treatment. The treatment is TMS. Vire's job is to say when to go back.
VireThe loop

The clinic sees who to call.

  • A well signature is set from pings in the last two weeks of the course.
  • Three low days in a row, or silence, moves a name up the list, with the reason in plain words.
  • One tap asks the person back for a booster session.

VireThe clinic

What is real today, and what is not.

Measured

1.2bpm

Camera pulse against a finger oximeter, on public recordings of 16 people sitting still. 28% of the time the signal is poor and it shows nothing.

97%/ 16%

Clear smiles found, and neutral faces wrongly called a smile, on public clips of 62 people.

8days

Median time for a fading echo to reach the clinic's early list, in Vire's own detector on 4,000 made-up people per setting, at 70% of days pinged. The cost: 1.5 to 4.3 false calls per person a year.

Not yet

  • No patient has used it and no clinic has seen it.
  • Nobody knows whether a fading echo comes before a relapse. The link to depression rests on a few small studies.
  • The sensor checks ran on a desktop, not a phone. Voice, head movement and AirPods are unchecked.
  • Vire doesn't diagnose and can't see a crisis. The app has one tap to 112 and the MIELI crisis line.
VireEvidence, and its limits

Next: one clinic, twenty patients, one question.

Does a person's echo move with their own symptoms?

  • 20 patients of a TMS clinic, pinging daily from the last two weeks of the course and for 12 weeks after.
  • A weekly PHQ-9 as the yardstick. The clinic does not see the echo.
  • The shape is TMS-EEG's. A pulse goes in, the brain's response is read, and in Aalto's lab that response steers the next pulse (Tervo 2022, healthy volunteers). Our pulse is a laugh, and a phone reads the answer.

The full protocol is on the evidence page for any group to pick up. Whether a smile and a brain response move together is untested.
