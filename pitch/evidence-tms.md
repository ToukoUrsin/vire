# Booster TMS after a course: what the studies say

Why this matters for Vire's outer loop: when drift lasts and the practices on the phone do not answer it, the clinic's list escalates the person, and the clinic can book a booster session. This page is about the evidence behind that last step, not about Vire.

## What is known

**Benefit fades for many people.** A meta-analysis of 19 studies found that of the people who responded to an acute TMS course, 66.5% still responded at 3 months, 52.9% at 6 months and 46.3% at 12 months (12 months: 9 studies, 247 patients). Studies with maintenance sessions had higher response rates over time. The authors say maintenance "may enhance the durability" and should be tested in trials (Senova 2019).

**In practice, many people get TMS again.** In a 42-practice observational study, 257 people who finished an acute course were followed for a year on continued medication. Of the 120 who responded or remitted, 75 (62.5%) still met response criteria through the year. 93 people (36.2%) had TMS reintroduced after the first month, for 16 treatment days on average (Dunner 2014).

**Bringing TMS back when people worsen often works, in open studies.** In a 24-week follow-up of 99 people on one antidepressant, 38 (38.4%) had symptoms come back (a 1-point worse CGI-S for 2 weeks). TMS was reintroduced, and 32 of the 38 (84.2%) regained benefit. 10 of 99 relapsed (Janicak 2010). This is the study closest to Vire's idea: watch for early worsening, then reintroduce TMS. It was open-label with no control group.

**Scheduled sessions vs waiting for symptoms: no clear winner.** A randomised open-label pilot (49 people, no antidepressants) compared one TMS session a month with watching and treating at relapse. Over a year the groups did not differ significantly, and only 16 people (32.7%) completed all 53 weeks. 78% and 63% responded to reintroduction (Philip 2016).

**Controlled trials are few and small.** A sham-controlled trial randomised 17 responders to active or sham maintenance for 11 months. Active was better between months 1 and 4 only (Benadhira 2017). In a 2025 trial, 75 responders received 24 weekly sessions or lithium. There was no difference at 24 weeks, 7 relapses in each group, and fewer side effects with TMS (Noda 2025).

**The overall picture.** A systematic review of "preservation TMS" (continuation, maintenance, relapse prevention, rescue) found 30 sources and 1,494 people. These included 4 randomised trials (1 sham-controlled), 14 open trials and 12 case series. It rated the quality of evidence for efficacy as low, and the support for effectiveness and safety as clear but mostly uncontrolled. Protocols vary widely (Wilson 2022).

## Strength of the evidence

- Durability after a course: moderate. One meta-analysis of mostly open follow-ups, with numbers that agree across studies.
- Reintroduction on early worsening helps most people who get it: low. Open-label studies only, no randomised comparison of "reintroduce early" against "reintroduce late" or "do nothing".
- Scheduled maintenance beats watchful waiting: not shown. One small open pilot found no difference, and sham-controlled data are one 17-person trial.
- Best schedule, dose or trigger: unknown.

## What Vire does not claim

- No patient has used Vire. The patients, practices, escalations, bookings and booster responses on the clinic screens are simulated, and they say so on screen.
- Vire does not show that a booster works, that it works better when booked earlier, or that its drift signal finds worsening. Nobody has tested that.
- Drift against the person's own usual is a signal for the clinic. It is not a diagnosis, and it is not a CGI-S or MADRS score. The studies above worked with clinician ratings, not phone signals.
- Booking a booster is the clinician's decision. Vire shows the timing, what was tried and what helped, and it books a slot only when the clinic clicks.
- The practices (breathing, body scan, walk) are short self-care exercises guided through the AirPods. They are not a treatment. The AirPods read head motion. They do not read brain activity.
