## Removed or softened, and why

1. **Girard 2014 is not a TMS study.** The treatment was an SSRI or interpersonal psychotherapy. The smile difference was significant in hand coding only, and it was an interview, not a stimulus. We say all three next to the claim.
2. **RADAR-MDD is not about pitch variability.** It found slower and quieter speech, and it compares across people. Our voice number (pitch variability) rests on Mundt 2007 (35 patients) and Mundt 2012 (105). CONTRACT.md should stop citing RADAR-MDD for pitch.
3. **Iseger's first results were healthy volunteers** (n = 10). The patient evidence is Iseger 2020 with iTBS (n = 15). We say which is which.
4. **The slow-breath test is not HRV.** The clinical test uses 6 breaths or more and beat-to-beat timing. We use 2 breaths and bpm. We call it a heart rate swing.
5. **AirPods give bpm during a workout session only.** We found no documented beat-to-beat data for apps. No HRV claim anywhere.
6. **ExpressEar's 42.1%** comes from a review, not the paper (paywalled). Marked as such.
7. **Kinetic Earables is smile and frown, controlled and not in conversation.** CONTRACT.md says "smile and talk ... sitting"; that wording is wrong and is not used.
8. **Kaur 2020** calls itself an independent replication, but two of Iseger's group are co-authors. We say "replicated", not "independently".
9. **The WARN-D ring is "a smart ring"** in the paper; no brand is named there, so we name none.
10. **Many Smiles** is used only to say the effect is small. We dropped any hint that smiling at a laugh is therapy.
11. **Doses.** The evidence is for whole programmes or for stress. We say a dose is not a treatment.
12. **Contagious laughter (Provine 1992, doi:10.3758/BF03330380).** Only the title could be opened, so it is cited nowhere. Whether the laugh still works on day 30 is listed as an open limit.
13. **No response rate for TMS** is stated anywhere ("for many people it works" was cut): we did not verify one.
14. **"Largest study" and "independent"** style superlatives were cut wherever the source did not say them.
15. **The Aalto brief names no researcher** and we have no link to the lab. Nothing in the texts suggests otherwise.
16. **Hall data** is described as tiredness over a weekend, never as anything about depression.
17. **Mundt 2012 did not repeat the pitch result.** Its full text shows the timing measures held up and pitch variability was not among the measures that separated responders. Our echo's voice number is pitch variability, so it rests on 35 patients. Said on the page and in Q5. Pauses and speaking rate are the better-supported voice features.
